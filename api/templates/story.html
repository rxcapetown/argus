Our story

Why Mashruf Habib — and why now.

Argus exists because the most valuable data in global trade was never written down — and the person to capture it is someone who has lived inside the shipment, not just the spreadsheet.

Why him

Seventeen years of operating where trade actually happens.

  • Founded an airline at 24. Regent Airways flew across 9 countries, carried an $800K/month logistics contract with Chevron, and ran in-house AI seat pricing back in 2014 — before it was fashionable.
  • Scaled apparel sourcing $3.5M → $52M for brands including Calvin Klein and Tommy Hilfiger — living inside the exact document flow Argus now structures: invoices, LCs, packing lists, inspections.
  • $800M+ in supply-chain GMV across 28 manufacturing units via Appnometry, his supply-chain platform.
  • Antler Founder in Residence (Riyadh, 2025); advisor to operators across fragrance and US–EU/Japan brand distribution.

He has been the shipper arguing with the bank over a $600 LC discrepancy. He has watched containers sit at transshipment ports while documents get amended. The exception taxonomy on this site wasn't designed from a textbook — it's the paperwork of his working life, formalized.

Why now

Models hit the judgment wall.

Frontier AI can read every trade document ever written. What it cannot do is make the call a veteran forwarder makes in thirty seconds: this value is wrong, this HS code gets held at the port, amend the invoice not the LC. That reasoning lives in people's heads. It was never captured as data — and the labs training the next generation of reasoning models are now starved for exactly this: verified human judgment, with decisions, evidence, and outcomes attached.

Meanwhile the supply side is sitting in plain sight. Thousands of freight forwarders and customs brokers make these judgment calls every day, on documents they already hold. Nobody built them a way to contribute that judgment without exposing their clients. That's the gap Argus fills: contributor-first redaction, expert cross-verification, and a fixed taxonomy — a human data supply chain for the AI that runs global trade.

The window is open because the demand (verified reasoning data) and the supply (practitioner judgment) matured at the same time. Argus is being built in that window, by someone who has stood on both sides of it.
