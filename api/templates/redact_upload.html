One dossier = one shipment, end to end: every document from booking to delivery, plus the expert's judgment on what went wrong, what it cost, and how it was resolved. If you can lay hands on these eight documents for a shipment, you can contribute a dossier.

The eight documents

  1. Commercial invoice — declared value, quantity, unit price, HS code, Incoterms
  2. Packing list — carton count, piece count, gross/net weight, dimensions
  3. Bill of lading — vessel/voyage, container & seal numbers, port dates, freight terms
  4. Certificate of origin — origin country, HS code as declared to the chamber
  5. Letter of credit (if applicable) — LC value, latest shipment date, tolerance clauses
  6. Insurance certificate — insured value, coverage terms
  7. Inspection certificate (if applicable) — inspected quantity, defect findings
  8. Export customs declaration — declared value & HS code as filed

Worked example synthetic

Dossier ARG-2026-04562 — men's cotton knit polos, 12,000 pcs / 8,400 kg, Ho Chi Minh City → Rotterdam, HTS 6105.10.00.

VALUE_MISMATCH

ComparedCommercial invoice INV-2291 × letter of credit LC-88412 (field 45A)
What was wrongInvoice total $48,500 exceeded the LC value of $47,900 by $600.
Expert decisionHold documents; have the shipper amend the invoice to the LC value (cheaper and faster than amending the LC).
Cost impact2-day document hold; $380 detention at transshipment port.
ResolutionAmended invoice issued 18 Mar; documents accepted; cargo released 20 Mar.
